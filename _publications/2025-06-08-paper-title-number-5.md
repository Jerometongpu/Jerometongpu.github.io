## Abstract

This article proposes a unified class of generalized location-scale mixture of multivariate elliptical distributions and studies integral stochastic orderings of random vectors following such distributions. Given a random vector Z, independent of X and Y, the scale parameter of this class of distributions is mixed with a function α(Z) and its skew parameter is mixed with another function β(Z). Sufficient (and necessary) conditions are established for stochastically comparing different random vectors stemming from this class of distributions by means of several stochastic orders including the usual stochastic order, convex order, increasing convex order, supermodular order, and some related linear orders. Two insightful assumptions for the density generators of elliptical distributions, aiming to control the generators’ tail, are provided to make stochastic comparisons among mixed-elliptical vectors. Some applications in applied probability and actuarial science are also provided as illustrations on the main findings.

## Overview

Elliptical distributions offer flexible tail behavior but are symmetric, making them restrictive when the joint risks or returns of interest are skewed. The paper introduces generalized location-scale mixtures of elliptical distributions, with separate functions of a common mixing vector controlling scale and the asymmetric location component. This construction includes several existing models, such as location-scale mixtures of elliptical distributions, mean-variance mixtures of normal distributions, and suitable skew-normal mixture models.

An expectation-difference identity provides the basis for comparing two members of this family through integral stochastic orders. The analysis separates sufficient parameter restrictions from necessary conditions, some of which require assumptions on the tails of the elliptical density generator. The orders examined include usual stochastic, convex, increasing convex, supermodular, upper orthant, directionally convex, and related linear or Hessian-based orders.

The applications show how these multivariate comparisons transfer to quantities used in probability and insurance. They include a generalized Slepian inequality, stochastic or increasing convex comparisons of aggregate claims in individual and collective risk models, and comparisons of maximum claim amounts. For portfolios with two or three components, additional conditions yield increasing convex comparisons of a Gini index, connecting changes in dependence to dispersion across the portfolio.
