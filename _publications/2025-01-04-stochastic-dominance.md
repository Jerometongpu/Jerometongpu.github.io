## Abstract

This paper investigates the stochastic dominance of claim spacings from heterogeneous insurance portfolios with (in)dependent claim occurrence probabilities and claim severities. We derive explicit distribution functions for the claim range and the second claim spacing. We then establish sufficient conditions for comparing claim ranges in the increasing convex order for two sets of heterogeneous insurance portfolios. Our results show that higher occurrence probabilities, characterized by stochastic arrangement increasing, together with larger claim severities under the conditional increasing convex order, lead to a larger claim range in the increasing convex order. We also show that, when claim indicators are independent, greater heterogeneity in the negative log-transforms of occurrence probabilities leads to a larger claim range in the increasing convex order. In addition, if claim severities are independent and follow proportional hazard rate models, greater heterogeneity in the associated parameters yields a larger claim range in the usual stochastic order. For the second claim spacing, we establish stochastic dominance results under the increasing concave order and the usual stochastic order for two sets of heterogeneous and (in)dependent portfolios. Several numerical examples are provided for illustration.

## Overview

In an individual risk model, each policy produces either no claim or a positive claim amount, so the claim distribution includes a mass at zero. This feature prevents standard results for continuous sample ranges and spacings from being applied directly. The paper derives distribution formulas for the claim range, the largest claim minus the smallest, and the second claim spacing, the second smallest claim minus the smallest, for heterogeneous insurance portfolios.

The comparisons use conditional stochastic orders to handle dependence in claim severities and stochastic arrangement increasing to handle dependence in claim indicators. Sufficient conditions establish increasing convex comparisons for claim ranges and increasing concave comparisons for second claim spacings. Further results connect heterogeneity in transformed occurrence probabilities and severity parameters with larger ranges. They obtain usual stochastic comparisons for claim ranges under independent proportional-hazard claim severities and for second claim spacings under independent exponential claim severities, subject to the stated occurrence and parameter conditions. Numerical examples illustrate the results and their limits, providing a theoretical basis for comparing portfolio dispersion beyond aggregate or extreme claim amounts.
