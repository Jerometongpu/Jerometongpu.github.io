## Abstract

The Vulnerability-CoVaR (VCoVaR), proposed by Waltz et al. (2022), quantifies the “tail risk” of financial positions by focusing on extreme losses that arise when one or more market participants experience financial distress exceeding their individual VaR thresholds. Building on this framework, this paper introduces another systemic risk measure, the Vulnerability-CoES (VCoES), which naturally generalizes VCoVaR to provide a more comprehensive assessment of tail-dependent systemic risk. We further develop a suite of contribution measures derived from VCoVaR and VCoES to systematically quantify absolute and relative systemic risk spillovers across interconnected entities. For comparative analysis, we establish sufficient conditions based on copula dependence structures and stochastic dominance principles, enabling systematic comparisons of vulnerability-conditional risk measures across distinct risk portfolios. Additionally, we propose backtesting methodologies tailored to VCoVaR and VCoES and apply them in an empirical study of cryptocurrency markets. The results provide evidence that these measures capture systemic risk dynamics and offer practical tools for monitoring financial vulnerabilities in high-risk environments.

## Overview

The paper studies the risk of a target institution under a vulnerability event: at least one among a selected group of other institutions exceeds its own Value-at-Risk threshold. It introduces Vulnerability-Conditional Expected Shortfall (VCoES) to assess the average severity of losses beyond the relevant conditional quantile. Contribution measures compare conditional risk with stand-alone risk, either as an absolute increment or after normalization, allowing spillovers to be assessed across assets with different risk scales.

Using copulas and stochastic orders, the analysis derives separate sufficient conditions for comparisons of conditional risk levels, absolute contributions, and relative contributions. These conditions account for the dependence-sensitive probability of the union distress event. The paper also develops backtesting procedures and applies a dynamic copula model to Bitcoin, Ethereum, Litecoin, Monero, and XRP. In this empirical study, VCoES highlights losses beyond the conditional quantile, contribution measures reveal differences in vulnerability across assets, and the reported backtests support satisfactory calibration for the sample examined.
