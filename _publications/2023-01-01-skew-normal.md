## Abstract

We establish an identity for E f(Y) − E f(X), when X and Y both have matrix variate skew-normal distributions and the function f satisfies some weak conditions. The characteristic function of matrix variate skew normal distribution is then derived. We then make use of it to derive some necessary and sufficient conditions for the comparison of matrix variate skew-normal distributions under six different orders, such as usual stochastic order, convex order, increasing convex order, upper orthant order, directionally convex order and supermodular order.

## Overview

Matrix-valued observations arise when several variables are recorded across multiple occasions or dimensions, and a skew-normal model allows their joint distribution to be asymmetric. This paper extends stochastic comparison results from skew-normal random vectors to random matrices, while retaining the row-and-column covariance structure of the matrix-variate model.

The central tools are an explicit characteristic function and an identity for the difference between expectations of a test function under two models. By expressing that difference through first and second derivatives, the analysis obtains necessary and/or sufficient parameter conditions for usual stochastic, convex, increasing convex, upper orthant, directionally convex, and supermodular orders. These conditions clarify the distinct roles of location, skewness, and scale or dependence in each comparison.

The results also connect matrix comparisons with weighted aggregate risks: suitable parameter inequalities imply stochastic ordering of sums of matrix entries with nonnegative weights. The increasing convex order has a stop-loss interpretation, while the dependence orders address how joint behavior changes when the marginal distributions are held fixed. The contribution is a set of analytic comparison criteria within the matrix-variate skew-normal family.
