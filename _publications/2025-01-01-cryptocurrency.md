## Abstract

Conditional risk measures and their associated risk contribution measures are commonly employed in finance and actuarial science for evaluating systemic risk and quantifying the effects of risk interactions. This paper introduces various types of contribution ratio measures based on the multivariate conditional value-at-risk (MCoVaR), multivariate conditional expected shortfall (MCoES), and multivariate marginal mean excess (MMME) studied in [34] (Ortega-Jiménez, P., Sordo, M., & Suárez-Llorens, A. (2021). Stochastic orders and multivariate measures of risk contagion. Insurance: Mathematics and Economics, vol. 96, 199–207) and [11] (Das, B., & Fasen-Hartmann, V. (2018). Risk contagion under regular variation and asymptotic tail independence. Journal of Multivariate Analysis 165(1), 194–215) to assess the relative effects of a single risk when other risks in a group are in distress. The properties of these contribution risk measures are examined, and sufficient conditions for comparing these measures between two sets of random vectors are established using univariate and multivariate stochastic orders and statistically dependent notions. Numerical examples are presented to validate these conditions. Finally, a real dataset from the cryptocurrency market is used to analyze the spillover effects through our proposed contribution measures.

## Overview

Systemic distress can involve several assets at once, and a large conditional loss does not by itself reveal the proportional increase over an asset’s usual risk. The paper builds contribution ratios from multivariate conditional value-at-risk (MCoVaR), multivariate conditional expected shortfall (MCoES) and multivariate marginal mean excess (MMME). It considers unconditional benchmarks and benchmarks based on median systemic stress, allowing relative contributions to be assessed under different reference conditions.

The theoretical analysis separates marginal risk characteristics from dependence among the assets. Using univariate and multivariate stochastic orders and copula assumptions, it establishes sufficient conditions for ranking the proposed measures across different risk portfolios. Numerical examples illustrate how those assumptions determine the direction of the comparisons.

A static empirical study fits marginal loss distributions and a Gaussian–Gumbel–Clayton mixture copula to daily Bitcoin, Ethereum and Monero prices from September 2015 to June 2024. The resulting conditional measures show larger losses than their unconditional benchmarks, while most measures place Ethereum at the highest risk level in the fitted sample. Differences between absolute and ratio-based results demonstrate how the benchmark and the chosen risk functional affect the interpretation of spillovers; the study evaluates joint loss exposure rather than causal transmission.
